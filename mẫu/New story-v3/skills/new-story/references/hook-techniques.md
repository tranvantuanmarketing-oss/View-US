# Hooks and reader-retention mechanics — one real exemplar per technique

Read in the `bible` step (plot-shape section) and the `caption` step (line 1 + reveal levels). Exemplars quoted from
`exemplars/en/` (number = comments). Do not copy exemplars; learn the mechanics.

## Line 1 — 5 `hook_type` kinds (generate 3, pick 1, record it in the bible)

Corpus of 37 posts: 0% open with weather, 31% open with dialogue, the rest open with "I" + an action, a time marker,
or an object. Line 1 ≤ 25 words, with a person and an action.

| hook_type | Mechanism | Exemplar |
|---|---|---|
| `dialogue-caps` | Cruel dialogue right in line 1, 1 ALL-CAPS phrase marks the sore spot | 04196: *"Try Not to Cry This Time," My Ex-Husband Mocked Before His Wedding* |
| `absurd-situation` | An absurd situation told straight, no commentary | 03780: *An elderly woman asked me to marry her as her dying wish.* |
| `confession` | The narrator confesses something they hid / do not understand | *I never told my son why I kept the second key.* (shape from exemplar 02551 "I kept my 30-year…") |
| `countdown` | An exact time creates a sense of evidence | 01780: *At exactly 3:07 a.m., my phone buzzed across the marble nightstand.* |
| `object-first` | A concrete object comes before the person | 03780 sentence 2: *her lawyer placed the old hospital bag she had guarded for years into my hands* |

## Curiosity gap — say there IS a secret, not what the secret is
Exemplar 03780 stops at "She chose you for a reason" — the bag is still unopened. The reader knows something is in the bag,
not what. The opposite fault: revealing what is in the bag already in the caption (the old toolkit often did this in line 2).

## Open loop in 2 levels — L1 and L2 are two different gaps
- L1 (caption): the object/document appears, unopened. Exemplar 05781 stops at the twins' birth certificate naming
  the narrator — one layer opened, the layer "what he does about Tessa" still remains.
- L2 (comment): opens layer 1, exposing a bigger layer 2 (who is behind it, a second document). The old toolkit often let the comment
  tell everything → no reason left to go to the web.
- Rule: each level = 1 new EVENT, not a new emotion.

## Dramatic irony — the reader knows before the character
Exemplar 05781: the reader realises the two blond children are his from the line "The same light hair I
had inherited from my father", 60 sentences before he dares to think it. How: give the physical
detail early and let the character not draw the conclusion.

## Poor-real details instead of rich details
Exemplar 05781: *worn sandals, a bag filled with aluminum cans, a folded twenty-dollar bill tossed
out the window*. Exemplar 03780: *orderly at a small nursing home, brought her tea after my shifts*.
Rich posts (01780: Beverly Hills, Peninsula hotel) still go viral but in a "them" voice, not an
"I" voice. The bible requires ≥ 2 poor-real details; source: the pack section "Everyday objects".

## Describe the body instead of naming the emotion
Exemplar 01780: *I stared at the screen for a long moment. Then I laughed. Not loudly. Not
hysterically.* — no "I was furious". Exemplar 05781 does the opposite ("something inside me broke",
"my blood turned cold") and is the one to avoid on this point. Lint blocks direct emotion-description phrases.

## Injustice → reversal — 8 plot shapes (old toolkit, kept)
Every story runs on 1 engine: **publicly humiliated → turned around by the truth**.
1. Lowly turns out to hold hidden power — the one thrown out turns out to be the owner.
2. Reunion through a trace object — blood relatives recognised through an object, a song, a recording.
3. Betrayal & asset stripping — losing the house/company right at the party.
4. A wronged woman/mother → cold revenge.
5. A child in danger → protected.
6. An unusual child / a miracle.
7. Exposing poisoning / abuse.
8. Switched child / inheritance (suits long series).
Three emotional pillars: outrage → satisfaction · heartache → warmth · shock → cut off. Pick the shape in the bible;
the caption shows only the "injustice" half, the web pays off the "reversal" half.

## Mildly unreliable narrator
One sentence admitting their own limits makes the voice more real: *I don't know how to explain it.* /
*I want to say I knew. I didn't.* / *I'm not proud of this part.* Use once per post, do not repeat.

## Countdown clock
Exemplar 01780 opens with 3:07 a.m.; exemplar 03780: *One week later… Three days after that…*. Concrete time
markers in the bible (`## Countdown`) force the character to act and give the comment/web
a deadline to hang on.

## Dialogue cut mid-line
At least 1 dialogue turn is interrupted by an action: *"You just what, Travis?" / "I didn't read it,
Mom. I didn't read it, okay?"* Cut = the speaker does not dare say it all = the reader fills it in.

## Sentence rhythm (from the measurements in `exemplars/en/_stats.md`)
Sentence p50 8 words, p90 20; 87% of paragraphs are 1-2 sentences; one sentence per line is the most common form. An effective
repeating pattern: *Not because X. Not because Y. But because Z.* (01780, 05781) — use at most once.
