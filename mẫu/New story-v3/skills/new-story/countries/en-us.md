## Translation tells (≥ 10)
1. Using "solicitor", "flat", "mum", "queue", "car park" → attorney, apartment, mom, line, parking lot.
2. European-style numbers "2,3 Millionen" / "1.200 $" → `2.3 million` / `$1,200`; the $ sign goes BEFORE the number.
3. "Mr./Mrs. + first name" ("Mrs. Linda") → Mrs. Miller or just Linda; strangers say "ma'am/sir".
4. Date "12/7" meaning 7 December → write December 7, or 12/7 = December 7 in US order.
5. Time "15:30" → 3:30 p.m.; "half three" is British.
6. "Notary" instead of a lawyer → in the US a notary only witnesses signatures; wills are attorney/probate work.
7. A "city hall" wedding as in Europe → courthouse / county clerk; church is still common.
8. Generic "hospital" for everything → ER, ICU, hospice, urgent care are different places.
9. Units: "kilometers", "kilos", "degrees" (Celsius) → miles, pounds, °F ("it was 95 out").
10. Dialogue too grammatical, no contractions → Americans say "gonna", "I don't know", "y'all" (South), "kinda".
11. "Cheers" for thanks, "brilliant" as praise → thanks, awesome/great.
12. Calling the mother-in-law "Mother" → "his mom", "Linda", or "my mother-in-law".
13. Inheritance written out in long words ("two million three hundred thousand dollars") → "$2.3 million"; in dialogue "two point three million".
14. Using "we" for the husband's whole family → Americans say "his family", "his side".
