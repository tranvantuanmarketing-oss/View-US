## Caption

"I married my high school sweetheart at 72 — two weeks after his children threw me out with only the dress I was wearing, his lawyer arrived at my trailer and said, ""Your husband made sure you got EXACTLY what you deserved.""

Garrett had kissed me behind the bleachers in 1972 and promised, ""Eleanor, someday I’ll buy you a diamond ring.""

Then life pulled us apart.

I married a good man. Garrett married a good woman. We buried them both, raised our families, and somehow got old without ever crossing paths again. Fifty-three years of that.

Then he found me at a church bake sale.

""You still wear your hair the same,"" he whispered.

""And you still talk too smoothly,"" I told him.

When Garrett asked me to marry him, I said yes.

He proposed three months later. I said yes — not for the mansion I'd never asked to see, not for money I didn't need.

But for the boy who once walked me home two miles in the rain and never once complained about his shoes.

Although his children never believed that.

Right there. Outside my trailer.

The attorney stepped out in a gray suit, holding a sealed envelope addressed to me.

""Eleanor,"" he said gently, ""I’m Garrett’s attorney. He left strict instructions that I deliver this to you personally to make sure you got EXACTLY WHAT YOU DESERVED.""
