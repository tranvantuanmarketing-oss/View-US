His mother stood beside him and nodded. “This family needs a grandson. Luckily, another woman has already given him what you never could.” Months later, at his wedding to the mistress who claimed she was carrying his baby boy, I arrived with my little girl in one arm and a sealed envelope in the other. The moment I revealed what was inside, the groom’s face drained of color. And that was only the beginning of my revenge.

The divorce papers h!t my face before sliding onto the hospital blanket wrapped around our newborn daughter.

Only two hours had passed since I named her Brinley, and my husband looked at her tiny face before dismissing her as worthless.

“I need a son,” Connor said, casually adjusting the cuffs of his shirt. “Not another burden dressed in pink.”

My body was still trembling after giving birth. Brinley slept peacefully against my chest, completely unaware that her own father had already rejected her.

Standing just behind Connor, his mother, Betsy, lifted her chin with quiet satisfaction.

“Our family needs a grandson,” she said. “Thankfully, someone else has already managed what you never could.”

At that moment, the hospital room door opened.

Connor’s assistant, Alyssa, walked inside with one hand resting over the curve of her stomach beneath a fitted dress. The smile on her face made it clear she believed she had already won.

“A boy,” she announced proudly. “Twelve weeks.”

For a split second, the room seemed to spin around me.

Still, I refused to give them the c0llapse they had come hoping to witness.

Connor tossed a pen onto my hospital tray.

“Sign the papers,” he said. “I’ll even let you stay in the apartment for three months. You should appreciate my generosity.”

My eyes dropped to the documents before returning to the man I had married six years earlier, back when he owned a single decent suit and more debt than savings.

Now he honestly believed the company, the luxury penthouse, and every prestigious invitation carrying his name existed because of his own talent.

He had completely forgotten who had opened every single one of those doors.

“Three months?” I asked quietly.

Betsy answered before Connor could.

“A woman who gives birth to a daughter should learn a little humility.”

I gently kissed Brinley’s forehead.

Then I signed only the acknowledgment confirming I had received the paperwork.

Connor never noticed the difference.

He laughed, leaned over to kiss Alyssa, then walked out with his mother following proudly behind him.

The instant the door shut, I pressed the nurse call button.

Twenty minutes later, my brother Parker stepped into the room.

He wasn't only my brother.

He was also a senior litigation partner at Davis Hardy, the law firm that had created my family trust and legally structured every company Connor mistakenly believed belonged to him.

Parker scanned the divorce papers once before looking up.

“He filed everything through company counsel.”

“I know.”

“That alone violates conflict-of-interest rules.”

“I know.”

“He’s also claiming the penthouse, the corporate shares, and the lake house as marital property.”

I lowered my eyes to Brinley’s tiny hand wrapped around my finger.

“They aren’t,” I answered calmly.

A hard edge settled into Parker’s voice.

“What do you want me to do?”

“Nothing dramatic.”

I looked back at him.

“Not yet.”

“Let him believe he’s already won.”

Three days later, Connor publicly announced his engagement to Alyssa across social media.

Betsy flooded her pages with blue balloons and proudly described the unborn baby as “the true heir to the family.”

Meanwhile, I sat quietly in my mother’s guesthouse just before sunrise, feeding Brinley while watching everything unfold.

Brinley had been conceived using our last frozen embryo.

Then an email arrived from our fertility clinic.

It was nothing more than a routine reminder.

Attached to it was Connor’s medical file confirming his permanent vasectomy had been completed fourteen months before Alyssa claimed she became pregnant.

I read the date twice.
